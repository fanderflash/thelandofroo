What Roo Looks Like

A northern land that feels familiar — until something impossibly old appears through the trees.

Roo is a northern country in Vanaheim, a little smaller than Sweden. Its broad geography is recognizable: forests, mountains, fjords, coasts, rivers, rapids, lakes, snow and tundra. But nature feels richer here. Trees, flowers, mushrooms and moss grow larger, denser and more vividly than in our world.

About the artwork: images on this site are visual interpretations of Roo, not architectural blueprints. Locked canon defines the world; concept imagery establishes atmosphere without fixing every tower, road or ruin in place.

The living north

Beautiful, cold and abundant

Roo should feel green and fertile where the climate allows it, with deep forests, dramatic water and mountains shaping long views. Farther north, the known land gives way to a colder region of snow and tundra that Rooans understand poorly.

Human scale

A medieval world of clans and roads

Farmers, warriors, merchants, priests and craftsmen inhabit a kingdom ruled by a king, jarls and clans. The human world should feel practical and lived-in rather than impossibly polished: halls, farms, harbors, temples, roads and fortified places built by people who still depend on land and sea.

The visual signature

TOO LARGE

The ruins break the scale of the world

Ancient structures across Roo were built on a scale too large for humans. A single stair step may rise nearly to a person's waist. Rooans therefore interpret them as works of the Vanir or other giant divine beings. Some are little more than remnants. Others form enormous complexes, and much of the ancient technology inside still functions even though humans do not understand the idea of a machine.

The sea

A country reconnecting

For nearly two thousand years Roo was isolated by dangerous seas after the moon disappeared. The sea became broadly navigable again roughly a century before the present story. The arrival of great ships from Varren now makes the coast feel like the edge of a much larger world.

Beyond Roo

The south should feel different

Varren lies far to the south across a large sea. Its broader continental culture is feudal, with noble houses, heraldry, castles and fortified cities, drawing creator-side inspiration from medieval England and France with some Roman influence. Highward itself is built directly over an enormous Vanir ruin.

The rule for future imagery

Roo should not look like generic high fantasy everywhere. The contrast matters: familiar northern human life beside things built for a scale and knowledge humanity no longer possesses. The ancient structures can be beautiful, overwhelming and mysterious without automatically looking evil. Hidden beings should remain rare enough that seeing one feels like an event rather than ordinary scenery.
