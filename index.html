World Guide

Roo & Vanaheim

Roo is a northern country in the vast realm of Vanaheim.

Its forests are deep, its mountain chains steep, its rivers fast and its coast broken by fjords and islands. Nature feels familiar but intensified: richer moss, larger flowers, enormous mushrooms, powerful waterfalls and landscapes that can look almost impossibly alive.

For nearly two thousand years, dangerous seas left Roo isolated from much of the wider world. Only within roughly the last century have the waters become broadly navigable again.

Vanaheim is much larger than Roo.

It contains many countries and several empires. Across the southern sea lies Varren, a powerful empire whose records preserve fragments of history that Roo itself barely remembers.

Roo is reconnecting with that wider world at the same time its internal order is beginning to fracture.

The Lore of Roo

Ten doors into the world

Welcome to Roo

Roo is beautiful, fertile and filled with remains of a civilization its people barely understand.

Read article

Roo is a northern kingdom shaped by centuries of isolation. Old ruins stand in forests, mountains and settlements, yet the knowledge required to understand them disappeared. Religion and folklore filled many of the gaps. Now rebellion grows, Arwen slavery is being challenged, foreign ships have arrived and the old structures are becoming important again.

Ruins of the Old World

Across Roo stand structures too large for humans and mechanisms nobody knows how to name.

Read article

The architecture is built on a scale that does not fit humanity. Steps can rise almost to the waist and arches tower above practical need. The strangest fact is that parts of the old technology still function. Treasure hunters loot them, priests interpret them spiritually, scholars study them and jarls sometimes turn them into forts.

The Moonless Sky

Around two thousand years ago, the moon disappeared and the seas became violently unstable.

Read article

The catastrophe survives under different names, including the Moonless Sky and the Time of Aegir. Waters rose, maritime routes collapsed and people believed Njord was punishing humanity. The seas eventually settled, but Roo remained isolated for nearly two millennia. The moon's disappearance is remembered. Its cause is not.

Varren & Highward

Across the sea lies an empire that remembers more of the old world.

Read article

Varren began as a kingdom and expanded into an empire whose emperor stands above conquered kings. Its capital, Highward, is built directly atop an enormous Vanir ruin. Deep beneath the city lies a machine Varren has never been able to fully activate.

The Tidal Gate

A colossal sea gate from an age nobody understands.

Read article

The Tidal Gate is a vast ancient lock or gate built into a wall-like structure between two seas. Its scale and surviving mechanisms are reminders that the builders of the old world possessed capabilities far beyond present humanity.

The Church of Drianor

A growing monotheistic faith centered on one of history's most famous figures.

Read article

Drianor tried to unite people after the ancient catastrophe and taught them to stop killing and stealing. He was later killed by an empire that still exists. The Church regards him as divine, though his true nature remains unknown. Drianor could activate and control Vanir machines.

The Dales

A region where rebellion is beginning to reshape Roo.

Read article

Jarl Resgar Vasa's resistance has destabilized royal authority and forced questions of loyalty, freedom and slavery into the open. The Dales are becoming one of the places where the old order of Roo starts to break.

The Magic of Roo

Six metaphysical principles shape all known magic.

Read article

Truth strengthens or weakens what is already real. Lie allows falsehood to function as reality. Dream reaches toward visions and time. Silence conceals. Ambiguity works through uncertainty. Unknown is associated with the Aesir and has no known inherent limit. Magical strength depends on discipline, life-force and conviction.

The Arwen of Roo

A people whose true origin has been lost.

Read article

Many Arwen in Roo are enslaved, though free Arwen also exist. They are generally darker-skinned, somewhat shorter than Rooans, robust and physically strong. Visitors from the southern continent notice a resemblance to continental peoples, but no one can identify their exact origin with certainty.

The Vanir Faith

The older religion of Roo sees a beautiful world diminished by the disappearance of its gods.

Read article

The Vanir are worshipped as gods while the Aesir are viewed as corrupt or demonic powers. Freyr, Freyja and Njord are believed to have been captured by the Aesir. Temples remain widespread, and ancient ruins are often interpreted through the language of faith.

The Story Begins

Book One

A kingdom at the end of its old world.

Roo has spent generations believing it is the center of its own story. Then rebellion grows in the Dales, strangers arrive from across the sea and ancient things begin to matter again. At the center are Fander and Morango, Arwen brothers whose journey collides with politics, old magic and ruins too large for humans.
